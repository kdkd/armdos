@X0F  A HISTORY OF THE ARM PIT@X07   @X08(as remembered by the sysop, who was there)@X07

@X0E1987 - The Pit@X07

  It started with a box that wasn't supposed to leave the lab.  A friend at
  Europa Micro Systems in Boca Raton, who still can't talk about it, lent me
  an engineering sample of a machine they were calling the ARM/AT.  Board
  revision "X2", serial number 00017, a hand-lettered label on the case that
  said DO NOT REMOVE FROM BLDG 3.  I removed it from building 3.

  I put it in the spare bedroom with a 10 MB disk, a 1200 baud modem and a
  second phone line my landlord still thinks is for a fax machine.  The
  board software was a pile of BASIC and hope.  I called it The Pit because
  that is what the spare bedroom looked like.  Handles were allowed.  We had
  a Captain Carrier, a NullModem Nancy, and a Phantom Dialer who never once
  left a message.  Eleven callers the first month.  I knew every one of them
  by the sound of their modem.

@X0E02-29-88 - The Night the Disk Died@X07

  Leap day.  At 2:10 AM the Florida East Coast freight rolled by, as it does
  every night, and the house shook, as it does every night.  This time the
  10 MB disk made a noise like a coffee grinder full of gravel and stopped.

  The message base was gone.  The user file came back from 38 floppies over
  one long weekend and four large pizzas.  My official position is that the
  crash happened on a day that doesn't exist, so technically it never
  happened.  The board was "down for a few weeks".  It was three months.

@X0E06-17-88 - The ARM Pit@X07

  The prototype went back to building 3 (I kept the keyboard; they never
  asked).  In its place came a real production ARM/AT, a 40 MB disk, a 2400
  baud modem, and a new name: The ARM Pit.  New rule, too: real names only.
  The old handles live on in the Wall of Fame (bulletin 7).

@X0E1989 - ARM-DOS 4.00, a dragon, and caller #1,000@X07

  ARM-DOS 4.00 arrived, and so did DOSSHELL, MEM and TERM in the box.  In
  August the first door opened: Legend of the RISC Dragon.  Productivity in
  South Florida dropped measurably.  In September Karen Whitfield became
  caller number 1,000 and won a coffee mug that says I AM CALLER #1000,
  which she claims to use every day.

  We joined FidoNet as node 1:1989/1.  Yes, I picked the net number to match
  the phone number.  No, you can't do that.  Mail goes out at 4 AM.

@X0E1990 - The Digital Line@X07

  On April 1st I announced our new DIGITAL LINE at 56,000 bits per second.
  Everyone laughed.  Bill Tran called from California to laugh personally.
  Look at the welcome screen and draw your own conclusions.  I'm not saying
  anything.  The cat isn't saying anything either.
