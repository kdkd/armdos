                               DRIVE D: IS YOURS
                               =================

  This drive is kept for you. Whatever you save on D: stays here, in this
  web browser, until you delete it yourself.

  C: is different. C: holds ARM-DOS itself, and it keeps your changes only
  until a new version of the C: disk comes out (new programs, fixes). Then
  C: starts over, fresh from the factory, and anything you changed or saved
  on C: is gone. New versions of ARM-DOS never touch D:, and neither does
  anything else on the web page.

  So keep your own things on D:: programs and games you bring in, your
  documents, BASIC and C programs, saved games. For example:

    MD D:\WORK                  a directory of your own
    COPY C:\TC\HELLO.C D:\WORK  a copy to change as you like
    XCOPY A:\ D:\FLOPPY\ /S     everything on a diskette, kept

  Programs keep their saved games next to themselves, on C:. Copy those to
  D: now and then, or install the whole program on D: and run it from there.

  D: is a 128 MB hard disk, the second drive in the machine. It costs your
  browser almost nothing until you fill it: only the parts of the disk that
  hold something are stored. The CD-ROM drive, which used to be D:, is E:.

KEEPING A COPY
--------------

  D: lives in this browser, on this computer. Another browser, or another
  computer, has a D: of its own. Clearing this site's data in your browser's
  settings erases it, and so would a browser that decides it needs the room
  (the page asks it not to). For a copy of your own, use "Back up D:"
  below the disk box on the web page: it saves the whole drive as one file.
  "Restore D:" puts such a file back, here or in another browser. The Files
  button copies single files between D: and your computer.

  You can FORMAT D:, CHKDSK it, DEFRAG it, even FDISK it: it is an ordinary
  hard disk. If you want it to start over, empty, "Erase D:" on the web page
  does that (it asks first).
